## What it is, and who it is for

The Autel MaxiCharger AC Elite Home is Autel Energy’s Level 2 home charger family for North America. The company sells 40 A plug-in models (NEMA 14-50 or NEMA 6-50) and 50 A hardwired models, each with a 25-foot SAE J1772 cable and a choice of in-body or separate connector holster. The Autel Charge app schedules and monitors the unit over Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or Ethernet; optional RFID cards can lock the station so only an authorized user can start a session.

It fits a homeowner who wants a smart charger with access control — useful for an outdoor driveway install or a shared parking spot — and who drives a J1772 vehicle or is willing to use an adapter on a NACS port. CNET named the 50 A model its security-features pick for that app-or-RFID lock. It is less of a fit if you want a native NACS cable (CNET’s stated con; Autel’s Elite Home datasheet and US product pages list J1772 only) or the simplest no-app box.

Autel also sells a related MaxiCharger AC Lite Home line. This page covers Elite Home only. We found a MotorTrend review whose URL still says “AC Elite Home” but whose current text discusses AC Lite Home at different list prices, so we do not cite that article for Elite specs or price.

This page does not assign a score. The verdict above is a synthesis of Autel’s datasheet and product pages plus the two independent reviews we read in full.

## Key specs (verified)

From Autel’s March 8, 2024 MaxiCharger AC Elite Home specifications PDF (linked from Autel Energy’s downloads page) and current US product pages unless noted.

- **Maximum output:** 40 A model **9.6 kW** (240 V AC × 40 A) or 50 A model **12 kW** (240 V AC × 50 A). Input/output voltage is listed as 208 V ±15% or 240 V ±15%, 60 Hz.
- **Configurable current:** Datasheet: output amperage adjustable via the mobile app **from 6 A to 40 A or 50 A** (to the model’s rating). Autel’s store page for the 50 A Elite lists input ratings of **16 / 24 / 32 / 40 / 48 / 50 A** and marketing copy that says “adjustable 16–50 A.” We treat the datasheet’s 6 A floor as the documented software range and flag the store’s 16 A wording as a mismatch.
- **Input / install:** 40 A — NEMA 14-50 or NEMA 6-50 plug-in. 50 A — hardwired only (datasheet part table and US product pages). The store’s 50 A page says a **70 A circuit** is required for full 50 A output.
- **Connector:** SAE J1772 only on the datasheet and current Elite Home product pages. Cable length **25 ft (7.5 m)**.
- **Holster:** In-body holster (integrated into the wall box) or a separate holster, sold as different SKUs. Datasheet model codes include `MAXI US AC W10-N14` / `W10-N6` (40 A plugs) and `MAXI US AC W12` (50 A hardwired), with `-H` variants for the in-body holster family.
- **Enclosure / outdoor rating:** **NEMA 4** on the datasheet and both Autel Energy US product pages we cited. Autel’s store page for the same 50 A Elite lists **IP65 / NEMA 4X**. We treat the datasheet and US product pages as the enclosure reference and flag the store mismatch.
- **Operating temperature:** **−40 °F to 131 °F (−40 °C to 55 °C)**. Storage: −40 °F to 185 °F (−40 °C to 85 °C) (datasheet).
- **Network:** Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, Ethernet, CAN, and RS485 (datasheet notes RS485 is for an energy meter connection). Status by LED and the app. Firmware updates over the air.
- **User authentication:** App, AutoStart, or RFID card. Card reader is optional (ISO 15693, ISO 14443, NFC). The store FAQ says RFID cards are **not included** and are sold separately.
- **Size and weight:** Datasheet outline **13″ × 7″ × 3″** (336 × 187 × 85 mm). US product pages give the same box as **13.23″ H × 7.36″ W × 3.35″ D**. The store lists in-body holster depth at **3.8″ (97 mm)** and separate-holster depth at **3.3″ (85 mm)**. Total weight **13–15.9 lb (5.9–7.2 kg)** depending on model (datasheet).
- **Mounting:** Wall, or floor using a pedestal (datasheet and store).
- **Safety / listings:** UL 2231-1, UL 2231-2, UL 2594, NEC Article 625, CSA C22.2; FCC Part 15 Class B; ENERGY STAR and OpenADR 2.0b (datasheet). US product pages and the store also say UL / CSA and ENERGY STAR. Older “Certification Pending May 2022” leftover copy still appears on the US product pages; we treat the datasheet’s listed certifications as current.
- **Warranty:** **36 months** (datasheet). US product pages and the store repeat a **3-year** warranty.
- **Load management:** The store’s 50 A Elite page documents Adaptive Load Management (ALM) and charger-to-charger Dynamic Load Balancing (DLB). It says ALM needs compatible extra hardware and DLB works between chargers without extra hardware. The March 2024 datasheet lists RS485 for an energy meter but does not use the ALM / DLB names.

Electrical note (our arithmetic from the store’s 70 A statement and the usual continuous-load rule, not a substitute for the installation manual): a 50 A EVSE is typically treated as a continuous load, so 125% of 50 A is 62.5 A and maps to a 70 A breaker. A 40 A plug-in setting typically maps to a 50 A circuit. Have a licensed electrician confirm this against Autel’s installation guide and local code.

## Smart features

Autel’s US product pages say the Autel Charge app can remotely start and stop charging, monitor sessions, and set a schedule. The datasheet adds Autel Charge Cloud, OTA updates, and app / AutoStart / RFID authentication. The store page says you can schedule around off-peak hours, adjust charging current, and enable RFID so unauthorized users cannot start a charge.

CNET liked that the station can be locked and unlocked with either the smartphone app or an RFID card, so an outdoor install can be protected while a visitor can still charge after being handed a card. CNET also capped output at 24 A over Wi-Fi to match an older home’s panel — which matches Autel’s documented adjustable current.

The Gadgeteer called the app extremely easy to use, with several ways to visualize charging data. The reviewer entered an off-peak electricity rate and let the app handle the rest. Notification volume was high until those alerts were tuned (for example, “charging suspended by vehicle” when the cars’ own schedules had not started yet). The Gadgeteer noted RFID lockout as a feature they did not test; they described it as useful for a small business or a charger that might be used when you are away.

## What independent reviews said

**CNET** (home EV charger guide titled for 2026; byline update May 11, 2024) named the Autel MaxiCharger AC Elite Home 50A “Best EV charger with security features.” Pros: “Up to 50A charging; available smartphone and RFID security; multiple installation options.” Con: “No native option for NACS connector.” The write-up calls it a solidly built and flexible home EVSE: hardwired or outlet-connected installation, built-in or external holster, and Wi-Fi control, monitoring, and current adjustment. The reviewer limited the unit to 24 A to stay within an older panel. Spec line: SAE J1772, 25 ft cable, up to 12 kW at 50 A / 240 V, 3-year warranty. CNET’s comparison table also lists the Elite Home as hardwired or plug-in, J1772, with a phone app.

**The Gadgeteer** (July 14, 2023; page updated March 19, 2024) reviewed the 40 A NEMA 14-50 model provided by Autel Energy. Daily charging was “flawless” at full 40 A speed. Likes: clean look and build quality, the NEMA 14-50 plug option, and a powerful app. The 25-foot cable was thinner than many rivals and easy to handle, though it kept some coil memory. The wall holster’s receiver rotates about 30 degrees. The reviewer charged two EVs (including a Tesla via a separately purchased adapter) and listed $559 as the price. The main hardware complaint was a very short NEMA input cord that forced awkward placement relative to the outlet. They also wished for a dual-EV model that could split a load — a request, not a tested feature.

A MotorTrend hands-on review originally published under an “AC Elite Home” URL now discusses the Autel MaxiCharger AC Lite Home and quotes Lite list prices that do not match Autel’s current Elite Home shop pages. We do not use that article as an Elite Home source.

## Honest trade-offs

**Documented strengths**

- 40 A plug-in (NEMA 14-50 or 6-50) or 50 A hardwired (12 kW) in one family, with app-adjustable current (datasheet; CNET used 24 A in a real panel).
- 25 ft J1772 cable; in-body or separate holster (datasheet, product pages, CNET, The Gadgeteer).
- App scheduling, session tracking, and optional RFID / app lock — the reason CNET gave it the security-features pick.
- Wide operating temperature (−40 °F to 131 °F), NEMA 4 outdoor rating on the datasheet, ENERGY STAR / UL / CSA listings, and a 3-year (36-month) warranty.
- Store documentation of ALM and charger-to-charger DLB for homes that may later add a second Autel unit.

**Documented drawbacks**

- No native NACS connector on Elite Home (datasheet, US product pages, CNET). Tesla or other NACS vehicles need an adapter (The Gadgeteer used one).
- The 50 A SKU is hardwired only; full 50 A output needs a 70 A circuit per Autel’s store page.
- Manufacturer pages disagree on enclosure rating (NEMA 4 vs IP65 / NEMA 4X) and on the app’s current floor (6 A vs 16 A). Use the datasheet with your electrician.
- The Gadgeteer found the NEMA plug’s input cord very short, which constrained where the box could hang relative to the outlet.
- Two official Autel shops disagree on the current 50 A selling price ($599 regular vs $525 sale).
- RFID cards are sold separately; The Gadgeteer did not test that lock.

Installation cost, utility rebates, and panel capacity are home-specific. None of these sources replace a licensed electrician.
