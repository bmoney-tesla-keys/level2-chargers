## What it is, and who it is for

The Emporia Classic is Emporia's original Level 2 home charger. When the company launched the Emporia Pro, it renamed this unit the Classic and kept selling both (Car and Driver). It comes as separate J1772 and NACS (Tesla-style) models, and you can install it with a NEMA plug or hardwire it.

It fits a homeowner whose panel already has room for a dedicated 240 V circuit and who wants smart scheduling and cost tracking at a lower price than ChargePoint or Tesla. Emporia itself says the Classic suits homes with "sufficient panel capacity," usually 150 A service or more (check with your electrician). For tighter panels, Emporia points buyers to the Pro, which ships with the Vue Energy Monitor and PowerSmart load management.

## Key specs (verified)

From Emporia's product page FAQ and Classic vs. Pro article unless noted.

- **Maximum output:** up to 48 A hardwired; 40 A on a NEMA 14-50 outlet. Car and Driver lists output as 6–48 A (1.4–11.5 kW).
- **Cable:** 25 ft with a remote holder. Car and Driver also lists 25 ft and an included wall mount.
- **Connector:** J1772 or NACS, sold as separate models.
- **Enclosure:** NEMA 4, for indoor or outdoor installs. Car and Driver also lists NEMA 4.
- **Certifications:** ENERGY STAR and UL certified.
- **Warranty:** 3 years.
- **Multiple chargers:** Emporia says its Intelligent Load Sharing lets several Emporia chargers share one circuit or sub-panel.
- **Load management:** PowerSmart comes standard on the Pro. On the Classic it needs a Vue Energy Monitor plus a firmware upgrade, at extra cost.

Electrical note (our arithmetic, not a manufacturer figure): EV charging counts as a continuous load, so a 48 A setting normally needs a 60 A breaker and a 40 A setting a 50 A breaker. Have a licensed electrician confirm this against Emporia's installation guide and local code.

## Smart features

Emporia says the Classic can automatically charge during off-peak hours. It also tracks what each charge costs and shows a charging calendar in the Emporia app. If you add a Vue monitor, the Classic gains peak-demand management and excess-solar charging.

Car and Driver says the app includes "detailed electricity pricing using your actual utility plan like the ChargePoint" and can link your EV through the Smartcar API. It also calls the app cluttered and "overly complex" if you only use it for charging. Another con in their review: you can't enter a specific vehicle to estimate miles or battery percentage gained.

## What independent reviews said

**Car and Driver** (hands-on test, updated November 2, 2026) named "Emporia Pro / Emporia Classic" its Best Overall pick and recommends the Classic if you don't need load balancing. Pros for the Classic: "Comparatively high output" and an "Attractive price point." Cons: "App is cluttered" and "Can't enter specific vehicles." The outlet also notes that a Classic plus a separately bought Vue 3 monitor ($529 combined in their text) gets the same load balancing as the Pro.

**Pocketables** (owner's running review, July 2026) bought a Classic, installed it on an existing NEMA 14-50 outlet, and found setup "a breeze." The reviewer lowered the charging amps in the app during heavy household load. Lessons from that review: order the right connector (they bought NACS by mistake for a J1772 car) and measure carefully, because they misjudged how far the 25-foot cable would reach.

## Price

Emporia lists the Classic from **$449** (J1772) and **$479** (NACS) on its product page, and its May 2025 comparison article gives the same $449 figure. Car and Driver's card also shows $449. Retail prices change, so check before you buy.

## Honest trade-offs

**Documented strengths**

- 48 A hardwired output at a price below most smart rivals (Emporia, Car and Driver).
- NEMA 4 enclosure, a 25 ft cable, ENERGY STAR, UL, and a 3-year warranty.
- Utility-rate cost tracking and off-peak scheduling.
- Can be upgraded to load management later with a Vue monitor.

**Documented drawbacks**

- The app covers Emporia's whole product line and can feel cluttered (Car and Driver; Car and Driver).
- No vehicle-specific range estimates (Car and Driver).
- Plug-in installs top out at 40 A; 48 A needs a hardwired install.
- Load management is not included out of the box; that is the Pro's job.

Installation cost, rebates, and panel capacity are home-specific. None of these sources replace a licensed electrician.
