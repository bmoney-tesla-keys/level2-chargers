## What it is, and who it is for

For years "JuiceBox" was one of the best-known names in home EV charging. It was a Wi-Fi Level 2 charger sold by Enel X Way. That company shut down its North American operations on **October 11, 2024**. VoltiE Group, a Florida-based EV charging company, announced in January 2025 that it had bought Enel X Way's US assets, including the JuiceBox line.

So "JuiceBox 48" now means two different things:

1. **The JuiceBox units already installed in homes.** They still charge. Their original Enel X Way app and cloud are gone, and VoltiE runs the replacement.
2. **New JuiceBox units sold by VoltiE** ("JuiceBox Powered by VoltiE"). They have a J1772 connector, come hardwired or plug-in, and are managed through VoltiE's PowerUp app.

This page fits two readers. One is a current JuiceBox owner who wants to know what still works. The other is a shopper who sees the JuiceBox name in older "best charger" lists and wonders whether it is still a good buy. It is not a hands-on review: we found no independent review of the VoltiE-era hardware.

## What happened to JuiceBox (verified timeline)

- **October 11, 2024:** Enel X Way USA closes. The date comes from VoltiE's January 17, 2025 acquisition announcement and EVinfo.net.
- **January 2025:** VoltiE emails JuiceBox owners asking them to register with its service. EV Club CT published the email on January 8, 2025 and noted it did not cover managed charging or a Wi-Fi-credential security flaw that Consumer Reports had raised with the FTC.
- **July 2025:** Per Consumer Reports, the old JuiceBox app "stopped working for most users in July 2025." To change settings, including maximum charging output, owners now have to use the VoltiE app.
- **November 14, 2025:** VoltiE writes that it bought the hardware inventory, customer account references, and device contact information. It says it did **not** take on Enel X Way's warranties or other liabilities, and that it moves residential units onto its network at no cost.

## Key specs (verified)

From VoltiE's two current product pages:

- **Connector:** SAE J1772 on both listings. We found no NACS version for sale on VoltiE's shop.
- **Hardwired model:** 32 A, 40 A, 48 A, or 80 A configurations. VoltiE recommends professional installation by a qualified electrician.
- **Plug-in model:** NEMA 14-50 plug, 32 A or 40 A configurations. It needs a properly installed 240 V NEMA 14-50 outlet.
- **Connectivity:** Wi-Fi, controlled through the VoltiE PowerUp app.
- **Store terms listed:** 30-day money-back guarantee; 2–3 business day shipping.

**Not published by VoltiE (left unknown here):** cable length, enclosure (NEMA/IP) rating, safety listing (UL/ETL), operating temperature, size and weight, and warranty length. The only warranty figure we found, "a two-year VoltiE warranty" on every new unit, comes from an EVinfo.net article labeled **Sponsored**, so treat it as VoltiE's claim.

For circuit sizing, Consumer Reports notes the JuiceBox Pro 48 "should be hardwired on a 60-amp circuit breaker." It also warns that a charger resetting to a higher default output than its circuit can handle could trip the breaker and create a fire risk. Have an electrician confirm the configured current matches your breaker.

Older reviews list specs for Enel X Way's JuiceBox. We do not copy them here because we could not confirm that VoltiE's new units share them.

## Smart features (VoltiE PowerUp app)

VoltiE's App Store listing says the PowerUp app can:

- show charger status (available, charging, offline, or waiting on a schedule)
- start and stop charging
- schedule charging by start and end time
- adjust charger current limits
- manage several chargers
- show session history with kWh and duration

VoltiE says app access and residential migration are free. The sponsored EVinfo.net article adds that optional paid features exist, that utility managed-charging programs are being reconnected one at a time, and that JuiceBox 1.x and 2.x hardware is supported. Charger IDs starting with 08, 091, or 092 map to those versions.

The old Enel X Way features CNET praised (Amazon Alexa control and the paid "JuiceNet Green" green-grid scheduling) belonged to the shut-down Enel X Way software. VoltiE's pages do not list them, so do not count on them.

## What independent sources said

**Consumer Reports** (Keith Barry, updated October 23, 2025) says installed JuiceBoxes still charge, but smart features "may not work or may be difficult to access" after the shutdown. It adds that the deadline for warranty claims has passed. CR points out that most EVs can schedule charging from the car itself if the VoltiE app does not work for you. It also mentions Juice Rescue, a community open-source hardware project, which CR says is aimed at tech-savvy hobbyists, and notes that modifications carry risk. CR's broader point is that many owners "may find it easier to just buy a new charger."

**EV Club CT** (January 8, 2025) reported VoltiE's first outreach email. It noted that Eversource's managed-charging partner was not integrated with VoltiE at the time, and that the email did not address the Wi-Fi credential security flaw Consumer Reports had reported to the FTC. We found no later source confirming whether VoltiE has fixed that flaw.

**CNET's** current home-charger guide still shows the Enel X Way JuiceBox 48 as "Best EV charger for green energy," with a $529 Amazon listing. The pros it lists are the green-grid scheduling software, the app, and Alexa integration. That entry describes Enel X Way software that no longer runs, so we treat it only as history, not as a current endorsement.

## Price

VoltiE lists both the hardwired and plug-in JuiceBox at **$499** for the 32 A configuration, plus **$50** for 40 A. The hardwired model adds **$100** for 48 A and **$200** for 80 A. That puts the 48 A hardwired unit, the closest match to "JuiceBox 48," at **$599**, which is the `approxPriceUsd` on this page. These are VoltiE's listed prices as of November 10, 2026. Installation is extra.

## Honest trade-offs

**Documented strengths**

- Existing JuiceBox owners can move to a free replacement app with scheduling and current-limit control (VoltiE; Consumer Reports confirms the VoltiE app is now how settings are changed).
- New units come hardwired up to 80 A or as a NEMA 14-50 plug-in (VoltiE).
- A 30-day money-back guarantee on VoltiE's store.

**Documented drawbacks and open questions**

- The original company is gone. Enel X Way warranties are not honored by VoltiE, and the claims deadline has passed (VoltiE, Consumer Reports).
- VoltiE's product pages leave out cable length, enclosure rating, safety listing, and warranty term.
- The 2-year warranty figure comes only from a sponsored article.
- J1772 only on VoltiE's current listings.
- Utility managed-charging support is still being reconnected program by program (EVinfo.net, sponsored; EV Club CT on Eversource in January 2025).
- No independent source we found confirms a fix for the Wi-Fi credential security issue reported by Consumer Reports.

Installation cost, utility rebates, and whether you need a panel upgrade depend on your home. Use a licensed electrician.
