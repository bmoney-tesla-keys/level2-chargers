## What it is, and who it is for

The Grizzl-E Classic is United Chargers’ no-frills Level 2 home EVSE (model family GR1-*). The company sells it as a simple, heavy-duty, portable station made in Canada, with a cast aluminum enclosure and a premium output cable. Colors on the product page include Classic Black and Avalanche White (the page also mentions Extreme Camo in the intro copy).

It fits a homeowner who already has (or will install) a NEMA 14-50 or 6-50 outlet, wants outdoor-ready hardware without an app, and prefers a lower price than smart chargers. CNET named it “Best EV charger value, especially if it’s going outdoors.” MotorTrend lists it under “other chargers worth checking out” as the bare-bones option when you want the cheapest trustworthy Level 2. It is less of a fit if you need built-in Wi-Fi scheduling or cost tracking on the EVSE itself, a native NACS cable, or the thinnest cord in the category.

This page does not assign a score. The verdict above is a synthesis of the manufacturer product page, the ENERGY STAR listing, and the two independent roundups we read in full.

## Key specs (verified)

From United Chargers’ US product page and the ENERGY STAR listing for GR1-14-24-PB unless noted.

- **Maximum output:** Manufacturer lists “40A 10 kW.” ENERGY STAR lists max nameplate output current **40.0 A** and maximum output power **9.6 kW** at 240 V (240 × 40 = 9.6). We treat 9.6 kW as the electrical nameplate figure and note that marketing often rounds to 10 kW.
- **Adjustable current:** CNET documents settings of 16 A, 24 A, 32 A, or 40 A at 240 V. The manufacturer Technical Specifications block on the product page lists adjustable current as 32 A, 24 A, and 16 A under a separate “Max power 40A 10 kW” line — we follow CNET’s four-step list for the full range and flag that the product-page field omits 40 A in the adjustable list.
- **Input / install:** Plug type NEMA 14-50 or NEMA 6-50 on the manufacturer page. CNET’s con for the Classic is “Plug-in only design,” and its comparison table lists install as plug-in. This page does not claim a hardwired SKU from the current product page we cited.
- **Connector:** SAE J1772. CNET and MotorTrend both treat Classic as J1772; NACS vehicles need a compatible adapter.
- **Cable length:** Manufacturer Technical Specifications: **25 ft (7.6 m)**. ENERGY STAR: **24 ft**. CNET’s narrative says a “nice and long 25-foot cable,” while its specs table and comparison chart say **24 ft**. We flag the 24 vs 25 ft mismatch and do not invent a single “official” length.
- **Enclosure / outdoor rating:** Manufacturer: UL Type 4 indoor/outdoor aluminum enclosure. CNET describes a dustproof, fire-resistant aluminum case built to **IP67** standards (immersion to one meter for 30 minutes in CNET’s wording). Car and Driver’s Grizzl-E Duo card (same brand family) lists **NEMA 4 / IP67**; that is the Duo, not a Classic hands-on card.
- **Network / smart:** Manufacturer Technical Specifications: “Smart function No Internet Connection.” ENERGY STAR: network connection types **None**. CNET and MotorTrend both describe Classic as no-app / no Wi-Fi. (The product page also shows marketing bullets about scheduling and monitoring that conflict with the Technical Specifications block; we follow the tech specs and independent reviews.)
- **Operating temperature:** −30 °C to +50 °C (−22 °F to 122 °F) on the manufacturer page.
- **Enclosure dimensions (EVSE):** 10.25″ × 6.25″ × 3.75″ on the manufacturer page. A separate “Dimensions” row on the same page lists larger shipping-style figures (about 13.4 × 9.5 × 15 in); we treat the 10.25 × 6.25 × 3.75 in line as the enclosure size.
- **Weight:** 10.00 kg / 22.05 lbs (manufacturer).
- **Certifications:** UL (manufacturer cites UL E510712); ENERGY STAR certified (listing dated August 6, 2024).
- **Warranty:** Manufacturer product page offers 3-year or optional 5-year warranty on new units. CNET lists a 3-year warranty. Refurbished units are described separately on the product page with 3- or 5-year options.

## Smart features

None on the Classic. There is no Wi-Fi, app, or onboard scheduling documented for this model. CNET prefers Classic over Grizzl-E Smart partly because the simpler unit “avoids many of the software issues users have experienced with the Smart.” MotorTrend calls it bare-bones with no app. Charge scheduling, if you need it, has to come from the vehicle’s own settings or a different EVSE.

## What independent reviews said

**CNET** named Grizzl-E Classic “Best EV charger value, especially if it’s going outdoors.” Pros: rugged construction, low cost, simple design that avoids software issues. Con: plug-in only design. Specs in CNET’s card: SAE J1772, up to 10 kW at 40 A / 240 V, 3-year warranty; cable length is 25 ft in the narrative and 24 ft in the table. CNET’s MSRP figure in that write-up is **$350**.

If you want the absolute cheapest Level 2 home charger that you can trust, this is it.”

## Honest trade-offs

**Documented strengths**

- Outdoor-oriented aluminum enclosure (UL Type 4; CNET’s IP67 description) and a wide operating temperature range.
- Adjustable 16–40 A output for different circuit ratings (CNET).
- No app complexity and a lower price than most smart Level 2 units (CNET, MotorTrend).
- ENERGY STAR and UL certifications; 3-year warranty on new units (optional 5-year on the product page).
- Made in Canada (manufacturer).

**Documented drawbacks**

- No internet connectivity or charger-side scheduling (manufacturer, ENERGY STAR, CNET, MotorTrend).
- J1772 only; NACS vehicles need an adapter.
- Thick cord (MotorTrend: 0.9 in) versus thinner cables on premium rivals.
- CNET flags plug-in-only design as a con versus units that also hardwire.
- Cable length and kilowatt marketing disagree slightly across sources (24 vs 25 ft; 9.6 vs 10 kW) — use the ENERGY STAR nameplate and an electrician for the circuit, not a marketing round number.

Installation cost, utility rebates, and panel capacity are home-specific. None of these sources replace a licensed electrician.
