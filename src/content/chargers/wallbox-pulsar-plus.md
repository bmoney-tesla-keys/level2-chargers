## What it is, and who it is for

Wallbox Pulsar Plus is Wallbox’s compact Level 2 home EVSE for North America (model family PUP1). The company sells 40 A and 48 A versions with a 25-foot cable and a choice of SAE J1772 or NACS connectors. The same Wallbox app schedules and monitors the unit over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.

It fits a homeowner who wants a small enclosure, app scheduling, and optional energy-management features (Power Boost load management, Power Sharing across multiple Pulsar Plus units, or solar charging) that need Wallbox’s add-on power meter. Car and Driver found the unit worked well after hands-on use but said it lacked above-and-beyond features versus cheaper top picks. MotorTrend called it a smart buy especially when panel capacity, multiple EVs, or home solar are in play. It is less of a fit if you want the lowest price in the category, or if you need nuanced variable utility-rate cost tracking without buying extra hardware — Car and Driver flags that gap versus ChargePoint and Emporia.

This page does not assign a score. The verdict above is a synthesis of Wallbox’s product pages and datasheet plus the two independent reviews we read in full.

## Key specs (verified)

From Wallbox’s August 2023 North America technical datasheet and current US product / shop pages unless noted.

- **Maximum output:** 40 A model **9.6 kW**; 48 A model **11.5 kW** at 240/208 V AC, 60 Hz (datasheet and product page).
- **Configurable current:** Datasheet lists software configuration from **6 A to rated current**. The later North America installation guide summary also documents a hardware current selector from 16 A to rated current (search snippet from Wallbox support PDF); we cite the datasheet’s 6 A–rated range as the documented software floor.
- **Input / install:** 40 A — NEMA 14-50 plug-in or hardwired. 48 A — hardwired only (datasheet, shop FAQ, MotorTrend).
- **Connector:** SAE J1772 or NACS, sold as separate configurations on the current US product and shop pages. The August 2023 datasheet listed J1772 only; NACS availability is from the current manufacturer pages.
- **Cable length:** **25 ft** (datasheet, product page, MotorTrend). MotorTrend measured roughly **0.7 in** diameter on the 40 A cable and **0.9 in** on the 48 A cable.
- **Enclosure / outdoor rating:** **NEMA Type 4** per UL 50E, indoor or outdoor (datasheet, shop FAQ).
- **Network:** Wi-Fi and Bluetooth; myWallbox app and portal; Halo RGB LED status (datasheet). Voice control with Amazon Alexa and Google Home listed on the datasheet.
- **Operating temperature:** Datasheet: **−22 °F to 104 °F (−30 °C to 40 °C)**. Shop FAQ marketing copy states −22 °F to 122 °F (−30 °C to 50 °C). We treat the datasheet as the electrical reference and flag the shop mismatch.
- **Storage temperature:** −40 °F to 158 °F (−40 °C to 70 °C) (datasheet).
- **Size and weight:** **7.8″ × 7.9″ × 3.9″** without cable; **4.4 lb** without cable (datasheet). Car and Driver described it as roughly an eight-by-eight-inch square and lighter/smaller than most.
- **Safety / listings:** UL 2594, UL 2231; ENERGY STAR certified (datasheet). Shop FAQ also cites UL and ENERGY STAR.
- **Warranty:** Shop collection and product marketing list a **3-year** warranty. We did not independently open a separate warranty PDF in this pass.

## Smart features

Wallbox’s US product page and datasheet say Pulsar Plus connects over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth and is managed in the Wallbox / myWallbox app. Documented capabilities include scheduling (including off-peak), remote status and control, user identification in the app, and electronic lock/unlock (MotorTrend). Datasheet included features list Power Sharing; product page documents Dynamic Load Management (Power Boost) and solar charging when a Wallbox Power Meter is added.

Car and Driver called the app clean and easy to use, with usual schedule features, but wrote that the pricing function lacks variable-rate tracking like ChargePoint and Emporia. They also noted Wallbox load sharing requires buying the company’s power meter, which they called “very pricy” at **$449** in that article. MotorTrend’s earlier review cited a **$279** power meter for Power Boost. Wallbox’s current US product page lists the Power Meter at **$299.00**. We do not invent a single “official” meter price — figures disagree across those three sources.

MotorTrend found the app attractive but called out a “bizarre” prominent amperage dial most owners should not need after install. Power Sharing can split one circuit across two or more Pulsar Plus units (MotorTrend says as many as 25). Solar charging on the product page requires the power meter and is described as incompatible with multiple-charger installations and OCPP.

## What independent reviews said

**Car and Driver** (hands-on; page updated November 2, 2026) described the Wallbox as lighter and smaller than most (about an eight-by-eight-inch square). Pros implied by their write-up: clean, easy app; schedule by variable rates or preference; load-sharing option. Cons they stated: pricing function lacks variable-rate tracking; load sharing needs Wallbox’s expensive power meter; overall, “it doesn’t offer any above-and-beyond features to justify a price that’s higher than that of our top-rated models,” though they found it worked well.

**MotorTrend** (January 4, 2023) recommended Pulsar Plus as a smart buy for panel limits, multiple EVs, or solar. They preferred the **$649** 40 A (9.6 kW, plug-in or hardwired, thinner ~0.7 in cable, rubberized handle) over the **$699** 48 A (11.5 kW, hardwired only, thicker ~0.9 in cable) unless you regularly fill a large battery from near empty. Pros: compact design, long cable, advanced features. Cons: quirky app, cheaper options if you do not need the full feature set. Their Power Boost / Power Sharing / solar examples match Wallbox’s current energy-management marketing.

CNET’s 2026 home EV charger guide (same roundup used on other directory pages) does not pick Pulsar Plus among its featured models, so this page does not cite CNET for Wallbox.

## Honest trade-offs

**Documented strengths**

- Very compact enclosure and light head unit (datasheet dimensions/weight; Car and Driver size comment).
- 25 ft cable; 40 A and 48 A power tiers; J1772 or NACS on current US pages.
- NEMA Type 4 outdoor rating and ENERGY STAR / UL listings (datasheet).
- App scheduling, remote control, Power Sharing, and optional Power Boost / solar with a Wallbox power meter (manufacturer; MotorTrend; Car and Driver).
- Plug-in NEMA 14-50 path on the 40 A model (datasheet, shop).

**Documented drawbacks**

- List price sits toward the higher end of home Level 2 units Car and Driver tested; they said it lacked justifying extras versus their top picks.
- Variable utility-rate cost tracking is weaker than ChargePoint / Emporia per Car and Driver.
- Full load management and solar modes need a separate Wallbox Power Meter; published meter prices disagree ($449 Car and Driver, $279 MotorTrend, $299 current Wallbox page).
- 48 A SKU is hardwired only; MotorTrend preferred the 40 A cable feel for most owners.
- Operating temperature upper limit disagrees between datasheet (104 °F) and shop FAQ (122 °F) — use the datasheet with your electrician.

Installation cost, utility rebates, and panel capacity are home-specific. None of these sources replace a licensed electrician.
